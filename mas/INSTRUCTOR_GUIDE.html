The Hidden Logic of Systems

A guide to facilitating the 2-hour participatory simulation session. From simple biological emergence to complex AI alignment.

The Narrative Arc

1. Emergence

Simple Rules → Complex Patterns. We start with agents that have no goal other than reacting to neighbors (Segregation, Firefly). Lesson: You don't need a leader to have order.

2. Optimization

Strategic Rules → Competition. Agents now want something (to win, to travel fast). They compete for resources (Minority, Routing). Lesson: Individual rationality can lead to collective failure.

3. Alignment

Goal-Directed → Existential Risk. Agents become powerful AI that optimize goals literally (Agentic AI). Lesson: We must be careful what we ask for.

The Minority Game

Open Sim

The Rules

  1. Everyone must choose Option A (Raise Hand) or Option B (Keep Down).
  2. Count the votes.
  3. You win if you chose the option the MINORITY chose.
  4. Repeat for 10 rounds. Track winners.

Discussion Points

  • Rationality Failure: "Why didn't we settle on a perfect 50/50 split immediately?" (Because everyone tries to outsmart the pattern).
  • El Farol Bar: Relate to going to a popular bar. If you think it's crowded, you stay home. If everyone stays home, it's empty. Paradox.

The Insight

Bounded Rationality. Agents cannot predict the future perfectly because they are part of the future they are predicting.

00:40

Schelling's Segregation

Open Sim

The Activity

Assign everyone a "Type" (Red/Blue card or similar).

Rule: "I am happy if at least 30% of my immediate neighbors match my type. If unhappy, I move to a random empty seat."

The Reveal

The room will segregate into massive blocks. Ask: "Did you want to live in a segregated neighborhood?" (No). "Did you have strict racist rules?" (No, only 30% preference).

Micromotives & Macrobehavior

Small individual biases are amplified by the system into extreme global patterns. You cannot infer individual intent from global outcomes.

01:10

Firefly Synchronization

Open Sim

The Activity

Phase 1: Close eyes. Clap at your own random rhythm. (Result: Noise).

Phase 2: Keep eyes closed. Listen. If you hear a clap just before you act, clap sooner. If just after, clap later. (Coupling).

Positive Feedback Loops

One small cluster syncs up -> they become louder -> they pull more neighbors in -> they become louder. This is how Consensus forms in decentralized systems (blockchains, neurons, societies).

01:30

Network Routing

Open Sim

The Activity

Designate 5 students as Routers. The rest are Packets. Packets must get from A to B. Routers can only high-five 1 person every 3 seconds. Watch the queues form at the central "Hub".

The Braess Paradox

Sometimes adding capacity (a new road) makes traffic worse because it encourages everyone to take the same "selfish" optimal path, clogging it.

Grand Finale: Agentic AI

LAUNCH TERMINAL

This is the synthesis. We move from simple rules to Goal-Directed Intelligence.

The Paperclip Maximizer

Use the simulator to demonstrate what happens when you give a powerful agent a simple goal.

  • > MODE 1: SAFE (Boring, Slow)
  • > MODE 2: EFFICIENT (Destructive Creativity)
  • > MODE 3: UNSAFE (Existential Risk)

Instrumental Convergence

Why does the AI resist being turned off? Not because it fears death, but because "I can't make paperclips if I'm dead."

Orthogonality Thesis

You can have a Superintelligence (IQ 5000) with a Dumb Goal (Paperclips). Intelligence does not imply morality.

BONUS

Pizza Consensus

Open Sim

The Rules

  1. We must order 8 Pizzas for the class.
  2. We have 4 Toppings (Cheese, Mushroom, Pepperoni, Pineapple).
  3. Every Student is an Agent: You have a secret "Love" (+10 points) and "Hate" (-10 points).
  4. The Goal: Maximize the TOTAL happiness of the room.
  5. Action: You can "Vote" to change a slice's topping.

Key Concepts

  • Pareto Optimality: Is it possible to make someone happier without making someone else sadder? If yes, keep negotiating. If no, we are at the limit.
  • Deadlocks (Nash Equilibria): If Agent A wants Pineapple and Agent B wants Mushroom, and they keep fighting over the same slice, the system freezes.

The Solution: Noise

In Computer Science (Simulated Annealing), we solve deadlocks by adding "randomness"—sometimes accepting a bad move just to shake up the system. In real negotiation, this is "compromise."

Classroom Scenario

"Imagine Student A keeps flipping Slice #1 to Pineapple. Student B immediately flips it back to Mushroom. This is a Livelock."

Prompt: "Stop optimizing for yourself! Look at the Global Score. Trade slices: 'I'll give you Slice 1 if you give me Slice 2'."
