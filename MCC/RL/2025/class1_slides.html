Reinforcement Learning in Modern AI

2/12

RL and Large Language Models

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has become fundamental in modern AI development, particularly in the context of Large Language Models (LLMs). The most significant application is Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), which has been crucial in aligning models like ChatGPT with human values and preferences.

Key Concepts in RLHF

  • Human Feedback Loop: Models learn from human preferences and evaluations
  • Reward Modeling: Creating reward functions based on human judgments
  • Policy Optimization: Fine-tuning models to maximize expected rewards
  • Alignment: Aligning model outputs with human values and intentions

The Prisoner's Dilemma

A fundamental example that demonstrates:

  • Individual rationality vs. collective optimality
  • The tension between cooperation and defection
  • The importance of repeated interactions

The Prisoner's Dilemma Matrix

5/12

Classic Prisoner's Dilemma

Player 2 Player 1 Cooperate Defect Cooperate (3,3) (0,5) Defect (5,0) (1,1)

In this classic version, the Nash equilibrium is (Defect, Defect) regardless of opponent's strategy.

Modified Prisoner's Dilemma

Player 2 Player 1 Cooperate Defect Cooperate (4,4) (0,6) Defect (6,0) (1,1)

In this modified version, with a biased opponent (70% Cooperate, 30% Defect), the Nash equilibrium becomes a mixed strategy.

Interactive Modified Prisoner's Dilemma Demo

Your opponent has a 70% chance of cooperating and 30% chance of defecting.

Expected Payoffs:

If you Cooperate: 0.7 × 4 + 0.3 × 0 = 2.8

If you Defect: 0.7 × 6 + 0.3 × 1 = 4.5

Therefore, the Nash equilibrium is to always Defect.

Nash Equilibrium

6/12

Definition

A set of strategies where no player can improve their payoff by unilaterally changing their strategy.

Properties

  • Self-enforcing: No player has incentive to deviate
  • May not be Pareto optimal: Better outcomes might exist
  • Can be multiple or non-existent: Complex strategic interactions

Tit-for-Tat Strategy

7/12

A simple but effective strategy in repeated games:

  1. Start by cooperating: Establish trust
  2. Copy the opponent's last move: Reciprocate behavior
  3. Forgive after opponent returns to cooperation: Maintain long-term cooperation
